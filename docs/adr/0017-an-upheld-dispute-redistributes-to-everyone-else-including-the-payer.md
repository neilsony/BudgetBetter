# An upheld Dispute redistributes to everyone else, including the payer

A Member may dispute their own Share, giving a reason. While the Dispute is open the Share counts against nobody — not the disputer, and not the payer who is owed it. Neither side's headline number quietly absorbs a contested amount; it sits in its own section on both dashboards, visible to both, with the reason attached.

The Owner resolves it three ways. **Denied** returns the Share exactly as it was. **Amended** sets a corrected amount. **Upheld** voids the Share entirely.

When an amount comes off a Share, somebody has to carry it, because the payer is genuinely out of pocket for the full total. It is split equally among every Member who had a live Share on that Expense apart from the disputer — **including the payer**, whose portion is self-owed and so is simply absorbed.

Worked through: $600 of utilities, six ways at $100, paid by the Owner. A Member disputes their $100 and it is upheld. The remaining $100 splits five ways at $20. Four Members now owe $120 each, the payer absorbs $20 against their own $100, and the disputer owes nothing. The Shares still sum to $600.

## Consequences

Excluding the payer was the obvious reading and is the one this rejects. Under it the payer recovers every dollar, and the people who had nothing to do with the wrong amount carry all of it — while the person who authored the split bears none of the cost of having got it wrong. It also creates a standing incentive problem: the Owner both resolves Disputes and holds Shares, so a rule that never touches the payer means an Owner who paid can uphold a Dispute at no cost to themselves.

Including the payer does not eliminate that — the Owner still decides — but it removes the structural free ride, and the resolution is visible to everyone either way.

A `Dispute resolution` Expense can never itself be disputed. Without that rule a resolution creates Shares, each of which could be disputed, each producing another resolution: the chain has no natural end.

An Owner may resolve a Dispute on their own Share. Forbidding it would leave the Owner's own Disputes permanently unresolvable, which is worse. Instead the timeline says so plainly — *resolved by the Owner, own dispute* — and transparency does the work a permission rule cannot.

Amending is expected to be the common outcome. Most real objections are "my share should be $40, not $100" rather than "I owe nothing", and without amendment the Owner is forced into an all-or-nothing call on a question that is rarely all-or-nothing.

## Considered Options

- **The payer absorbs the whole upheld amount**: rejected — defensible, since they authored the split, but it makes raising a Dispute costless and correcting one very expensive, which pushes payers toward denying everything.
- **Redistributing to the other owers but not the payer**: rejected as described above.
- **Voiding the whole Expense when any Share is disputed**: rejected — one person's objection should not unwind five other people's settled Shares.
- **Approve or deny only**: rejected once it was clear amendment is the shape most real disputes take.
